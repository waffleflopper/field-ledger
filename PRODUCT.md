# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Field Ledger is for an individual Army property holder managing personal hand
receipts, property items, active 2062s, locations, assignees, and recurring item
requirements. The user is often working near the property itself, on a phone,
tablet, or work computer, and needs fast answers without setting up an
organization workspace.

The primary job is property accountability: knowing what property the user has,
where it is, who it is signed to, which 2062s are active, and what item-level
requirements are overdue or coming due.

## Product Purpose

Field Ledger is a mobile-first personal property-accountability tool. It helps
one owner account manage many hand receipts while preserving the boundary that
official records and regulations remain outside the app.

Success looks like a calm, serious work surface that lets a user act quickly in
the field, review accountable records with confidence, and understand the state
of their property without turning the workflow into a spreadsheet or a fake unit
system.

## Brand Personality

Field Ledger should feel sturdy, calm, and accountable.

The voice is plain and operational. It should use Army-adjacent vocabulary when
that vocabulary is the correct workflow truth, but it should avoid pretending to
be an official Army system. The product should feel like a trusted pocket
notebook with good memory: practical, legible, and strict where records matter.

## Anti-references

- Do not look like a generic SaaS analytics dashboard.
- Do not use purple-gradient AI styling, neon tech styling, or decorative
  glassmorphism.
- Do not make the product feel like a unit, organization, commander, PBO, or
  clinic workspace.
- Do not imply Field Ledger is an official system of record.
- Do not create placeholder mega-routes or fake product workflows.
- Do not center the experience on item photos, reports, tags, or analytics.

## Design Principles

1. **Operational truth before decoration.** Navigation, labels, and hierarchy
   should reflect real property-accountability workflows: Dashboard, Items, Hand
   Receipts, Active 2062s, Activity, Settings, and Billing.
2. **Fast in the field, composed at the desk.** Phone layouts prioritize thumb
   reach and scanning. Tablet and desktop layouts use the extra space for
   comparison, detail, and sidebar navigation.
3. **Accountability leaves a trail.** Meaningful state changes should feel
   preserved, reviewable, and reversible where possible. Archive, close, and
   pause are preferred over hard delete.
4. **Honest boundaries.** The interface should plainly communicate what the app
   is not: not official recordkeeping, not classified storage, not PHI, and not
   sensitive operational planning.
5. **Dense, but never noisy.** The app should support repeated use and record
   review without turning every surface into cards, stats, or decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, and reduced
motion. The UI must remain usable outdoors, in office lighting, and on smaller
phone screens. Color cannot be the only indicator for status or risk; labels,
icons, and text must carry meaning as well.
